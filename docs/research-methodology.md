# Research methodology

Scripture is the theological foundation. Historical evidence, traditions and interpretations have distinct roles and must not be conflated. A source can establish that a claim was made without establishing that the claim is true.

## Entry requirements

Use a permanent identifier, title, summary, evidence classification, confidence, review status, specific citations and related entry identifiers. For named entities add aliases, original-language terminology, earliest identifiable attestation and competing interpretations when supported.

The reference register records source type, author or responsible body, title, edition or translation when relevant, publication date, access date, locator and URL where applicable. Use passage-level or page-level locators in each claim citation. Never invent missing metadata; distinguish unknown and not applicable.

## Evidence
- Scripturally Attested: explicit textual support.
- Historical Source: identifiable historical documentation.
- Theological Interpretation: inference or theological analysis.
- Extra-Biblical Tradition: non-canonical tradition or folklore.
- Unverified Report: attributed allegation without sufficient corroboration.

Confidence is a separate field. Owner-approved content is identified as such where approval has been given. Subsequent research may be published under standing authorisation without claiming an individual owner review. Correct errors transparently; publication approval does not alter evidence classifications or confidence.

## Publication
Provide context, alternative interpretations where material, and a bibliography. Protect privacy and distinguish medical diagnoses from theological claims. Do not publish practical occult procedures or dangerous exorcism instructions. Verify rights before reproducing copyrighted text or imagery.
